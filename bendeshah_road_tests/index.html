Flagship Technical Road Test · Project 01

AgriNexus — Walkaround, Test Route & Telemetry

CHECK ENGINE: EXTERNAL SHIFT DOCUMENTED

What problem is this solving?

Most plant-disease vision models are trained and tested on clean, isolated leaf photos—then deployed where users upload random non-leaf photos (soil, hands, tools) or field images under harsh lighting. Without an upstream gate, a softmax classifier confidently assigns a plant disease to a non-leaf object, and single-split validation scores hide out-of-distribution drops.

Under the hood

AgriNexus pairs a ConvNeXt plant disease classifier with an explicit upstream reject-non-leaf gate. Every input is checked for relevance before classification, and the system is evaluated on both its validation split (97.65%) and an external benchmark (42.86%).

⚠ Known limits

97.65% validation accuracy and 42.86% external benchmark accuracy measure two very different things.

Validation accuracy reflects performance on images drawn from the same distribution as the training set. When tested on an external benchmark with different lighting, backgrounds, and capture conditions, accuracy drops to 42.86%. Documenting both numbers openly is essential because real-world deployment looks like the external benchmark, not the validation split.

SECTOR 04 · WORKSHOP & BUILD LOG
Build Log / Notes

Build Log

Honest workshop observations on machine learning evaluation, failed assumptions, privacy-aware tools, and documenting work in progress.

Test result ENTRY 01

Why validation results can mislead

A 97.65% validation score looks great on paper, just like a brochure fuel-economy figure tested on a flat indoor roller. Taking AgriNexus onto an external benchmark (42.86%) exposed how quickly domain shift—lighting, background clutter, and camera differences—cuts into real-world accuracy.

Workshop observation ENTRY 02

What failed before adding the reject-non-leaf gate

Standard image classifiers are forced to pick a class even when handed a completely irrelevant photo. Adding an upstream reject-non-leaf gate stopped invalid uploads at the intake stage before the ConvNeXt classifier could hallucinate a plant diagnosis.

Owner’s note ENTRY 03

Debugging assumptions in reward pipelines

While building EcoSnap, the core engineering lesson was that any reward loop invites spoofing. Treating every disposal upload as an untrusted input that must clear an anti-fraud pipeline kept the system grounded in reality.

Owner’s note ENTRY 04

Why privacy-aware tooling is an engineering discipline

Good tools collect only what is strictly needed to verify a task and avoid hoarding raw user data by default. Privacy isn’t a legal footer added at the end—it’s an architectural decision made at the start.

Still investigating ENTRY 05

Documenting unfinished work without fake numbers

Not every project is at the same stage. Keeping Smart Scan for Electronic Warfare clearly labeled as an SIH idea IN PROGRESS and Video Pipeline as a smaller build makes the completed evidence on AgriNexus and EcoSnap trustworthy.

Workshop observation ENTRY 06

Learning in public means keeping the failure logs

Whenever a test run drops from 97.65% to 42.86%, that gap is the most valuable part of the project. Writing down exactly what broke turns a disappointing benchmark into a concrete engineering checklist.
